## Q&A

### Tax on Hong Kong policies

| Question | One-line answer |
|---|---|
| [My agent left the company — who handles my policy now?](qa/agent-left-who-services-my-policy.md) | **The contract was never with the agent.** Art. 127(1): acts within authority are **the insurer's responsibility**; Art. 127(2) covers even acts **after authority terminated** where the policyholder had reason to believe it existed — the insurer pursues the agent, **not the buyer**. Departure is not among Art. 36's enumerated grounds for suspension. ⚠️ Settles **responsibility, not servicing** — no primary authority found on assigning replacement staff. **You never needed a particular individual to exercise your rights** |
| [My insurer discontinued the product — does that affect my policy?](qa/product-discontinued-what-happens.md) | **Discontinuation stops new business; it does not amend contracts in force.** Art. 36 confines suspension to premium default — discontinuation is not among the statutory grounds. Short-term health insurance carries a specific duty: continue cover through the policy period, offer transfer guidance at expiry, disclose 30 days ahead (3 days if regulator-ordered). ⚠️ "Transfer guidance" is a service obligation, **not an undertaking to underwrite**. ⚠️ Scope is short-term health insurance only |
| [Missed a premium payment — does the policy just die?](qa/cannot-pay-premium-what-happens.md) | **Not immediately.** PRC Insurance Law Art. 36 builds in a buffer — suspension follows only 30 days after the insurer's demand or 60 days past the agreed period — and **claims inside that window are still payable**, less the unpaid premium. Art. 37 allows reinstatement within two years, **but it requires the insurer's agreement** and commonly fresh health disclosure, so "reinstatement is guaranteed within two years" is imprecise. The dangerous stage is suspension: the policy exists but does not respond. Chapter II Section 2 — personal insurance only |
| [Are Hong Kong policy proceeds taxed at 20% in mainland China?](qa/hk-policy-dividend-tax-2026.md) | A policy does not hold one kind of money — it holds four. Claim proceeds are statutorily exempt (IIT Law Art. 4(5)); dividends and the surrender gain are unsettled in doctrine; prepaid-premium interest is the most clearly characterisable. Caixin (5 Aug 2026) reports Beijing and Hangzhou cases taxed at 20%, while stating this "is not yet general practice" |
| [Is a withdrawal from a Hong Kong savings policy taxed?](qa/hk-savings-policy-withdrawal-tax.md) | Turns on **which kind of money you take**: claim proceeds exempt; value held and not withdrawn is unrealised; the gain above cumulative premiums on surrender is unsettled. **Return of principal is never income** |

### CRS, reporting and enforcement

| Question | One-line answer |
|---|---|
| [Has China started CRS back-tax enforcement?](qa/crs-back-tax-enforcement-china.md) | Yes — a published five-step reconciliation of exchanged data against filings; published cases RMB 510k–6.99m. Voluntary correction = back tax + surcharge; audit-stage evasion = 0.5×–5× fines. Driver is CRS 1.0 stock data. **Reporting is not taxation** |
| [Does CRS 2.0 report my e-wallet, and from which year?](qa/crs-2-0-scope-and-timeline.md) | Scope: yes (specified e-money, CBDCs, indirect crypto exposure). But "live in 2026" is only first-wave jurisdictions; Hong Kong proposes CARF 2027 / amended CRS 2028 (bill still before LegCo); mainland China has no official timeline |
| [What is the penalty for a false CRS self-certification in Hong Kong?](qa/crs-false-self-certification-penalty.md) | **No imprisonment.** IRO s.80(2E) — a level-3 fine of HK$10,000. The "6 months / HK$50,000" figure belongs to the separate evasion offences under ss.80B–80F and is misattributed by rumour |
| [Hong Kong's two "automatic exchange" bills of 2026 — which one passed on 17 June?](qa/hong-kong-two-aeoi-bills-2026.md) | ⚠️ **"Passed 17 June = crypto now reported" conflates two separate bills.** The one passed strengthens **enforcement** (registration, records, penalties; effective 1 Jan 2027) and adds no asset classes. The one still before LegCo widens **scope** (CARF + CRS 2.0) |
| [Outbound-investment filing vs CRS reporting — what's the difference?](qa/outbound-filing-vs-crs-reporting.md) | Filing governs money **leaving** China; CRS governs assets **already offshore**. Two different doors — neither exempts the other |
| [Do existing Hong Kong policies need filing under Decree 837?](qa/decree-837-existing-hk-policies.md) | **No — there is no retroactive clause.** Existing holdings are safe; new flows follow the forthcoming individual rules |

### Choosing an insurer, and reading what they publish

| Question | One-line answer |
|---|---|
| [Can you compare Hong Kong insurers by solvency ratio?](qa/hk-insurer-solvency-ratio-how-to-read.md) | ⚠️ **Much material online still quotes a 150% minimum — superseded by the risk-based capital regime on 1 July 2024.** A solvency ratio is a point-in-time figure, not a league table: one insurer lawfully reported 337% and 289% on the same day under two regimes. Read four dimensions instead |
| [Hong Kong insurance's three public numbers](qa/hk-insurance-three-numbers-check.md) | Three numbers, three questions: solvency answers "will it stand"; fulfilment ratios answer "were illustrations honoured"; illustration caps (6.0% HKD / 6.5% non-HKD) answer "how high may the projection be drawn". No single gauge is the verdict |
| [Reading GL16's unified fulfilment-ratio disclosure](qa/gl16-fulfilment-ratio-disclosure-guide.md) | **There is no absolute pass-line.** Read three dimensions — length of series × like-for-like product basis × your own objective. GL34's participating-fund segregation is a governance safeguard, not bankruptcy remoteness |
| [Is a Hong Kong participating (dividend) policy reliable?](qa/hk-participating-policy-two-metric-check.md) | Two metrics answering two questions — fulfilment ratio (long-run delivery on illustrations) and solvency (financial strength). Dividends are **non-guaranteed illustrations**, not promises |

| [Will the CI definition standardization (1 Sep 2026) affect my policy?](qa/hk-ci-definition-standardization-2026.md) | **Existing policies are not affected — clauses 6.1 and 6.3 say so.** Hold three qualifiers from the text: **voluntary** (2.2), **minimum standard** (3.2), **new products only** (6.1). **Renewal is the one exception** worth confirming in writing (6.2) |

### Accident cover

| Question | One-line answer |
|---|---|
| [My accident claim was denied — why?](qa/accident-insurance-occupation-class.md) | The first gate is **occupation class**, not the sum insured — and it is assessed **twice**: at underwriting against what you *declared*, at claim against what you were *actually doing*. **No national classification table exists**, so "occupation X is class N" is incomplete without naming a product. **Class drift** — your work changes, your class moves, and if it leaves the covered range unnotified the policy stops responding **with no warning of any kind** |

### Moving back to China, and checking what you are told

| Question | One-line answer |
|---|---|
| [Returning after study abroad — how long until medical insurance pays?](qa/returnee-medical-insurance-waiting-period.md) | **The rule does not reach back before 2025** — years uninsured abroad before then do not count, so the four-year/six-month floor is **first possible in 2029**. Most returnees' three months comes from the word "**or**": enrolling outside the concentrated window triggers it regardless of years abroad. **Timing is the free variable** |
| [Is that comparison table comparing the product I am buying?](qa/product-comparison-table-version-check.md) | **The table may be entirely correct.** It lists a **brand**; you buy a **version** — one series often holds several, differing in benefit term, riders or premium structure. Two figures settle it: **annual premium + benefit term**. "Premium matches, returns differ" signals different issue dates — guaranteed figures are contractual, projected ones update **Includes a measured set of six**: three agree to within 0.1%, three deviate 3%–11%, and the two groups fall out cleanly — **the shape of the distribution is the evidence** (computation error distributes randomly; it does not group). |
| [Is my insurance premium guaranteed?](qa/is-my-insurance-premium-guaranteed.md) | **"Annual premium × payment term = total outlay" holds only where the rate is guaranteed.** Where it is not, the figure shown is a **first-year price, not a long-run cost**, and is not comparable with a guaranteed-rate product's same column. Two places to look: a **dedicated field** in the first-page summary, or a **numbered footnote**. Then read four facets — frequency / trigger / method ("uniformly, by risk class" is **favourable to the policyholder**) / any cap. **The one variable that changes every other number — and most comparison tables have no column for it** **Includes a measured set of six**: five verifiable, **only one guaranteed** — and the one guaranteed product puts it in a first-page field while all four others use footnotes; each of the four discloses only one facet (frequency / trigger / method / indeterminacy), so **only reading across gives the full shape**. |
| [Can you still buy insurance with nodules, stones, or a chronic condition?](qa/pre-existing-conditions-and-underwriting.md) | **Insurability is an underwriting decision** — nobody can give it without your reports. Three things are settled: **①the questionnaire defines disclosure** (inquiry-based: answer what is asked, no duty to volunteer what is not); **②five outcomes, not two** — standard / loading / **exclusion** / postponement / decline, and **exclusion means the contract is formed and all other cover is effective**; **③two-year incontestability favours applicants but is not unconditional** — disputes concentrate on **the event having already occurred at application ＋ deliberate concealment**. ⚠️ **A nodule is a health abnormality, not "an insured event that has already occurred."** ⚠️ One decline ≠ the market declines you |
| [How much do you actually get back if you surrender a life policy?](qa/how-much-do-you-get-back-when-you-surrender.md) | **Surrender pays the cash value, not the premiums you paid.** PRC Insurance Law **Article 47**: refunded within 30 days, **as provided in the contract**. The early-year gap is **structural, not a penalty** — premiums amortise acquisition costs and pay for protection already received. **Every year's figure is fixed in the cash value table at issuance.** Three ways to get funds without surrendering: **reduce sum assured** (permanent), **policy loan** (interest, reduces payout), **partial withdrawal** (some products). For participating policies the guaranteed portion is verifiable; **on the non-guaranteed portion nobody can give a definite figure**. Do this in 30 seconds: **open the table, read this year and the year it exceeds premiums paid** |
| [Someone is using an effective date to press me to buy. Is that a reason?](qa/is-a-deadline-a-reason-to-buy-insurance.md) | **A date is a public fact, not a personal reason** — it applies identically to everyone, while "should I act now" differs for everyone, and **a fact identical for all cannot explain a conclusion that differs between them**. Four claims, four places to check: price rises → **pricing provision**; "rules take effect" → **issuing body and mode of adoption** (self-regulation ≠ mandate; **misjudge this and every later inference inherits the error**); "all the same" → **scope qualifier ＋ minimum-standard status**; "stricter" → whether differences are **enumerated**. ⚠️ **In-force and renewal differ in strength.** Test: **remove the date and ask again** |
| [What remains of a critical illness policy after it has paid?](qa/what-remains-after-a-critical-illness-claim.md) | A claim is **not the end of the contract** — it is where the contract **changes shape and carries on**. One claim alters **five things at once**: future premiums, death benefit, cash/surrender value, terminal dividend, subsequent illness cover. Most early-stage payments are an **advance against one limit, not an addition**; a minority carry **restoration**. **Whether a death benefit survives is a difference of kind, not degree.** Look in the **termination clause** and the **numbered notes**; search *waiver*, *terminate*, *death*. ⚠️ "Cover to age 100" and "subsequent claims to age 85" are often two figures on one document **Includes three opposite-running differences across six products**: whether a death benefit survives is a difference of kind (one retains 20% of the sum insured, two state it is not provided); after a partial claim one product's premium falls and another's expressly does not; five reduce the limit one way only while one restores it. |
| [How do I check an insurance figure someone gave me?](qa/how-to-verify-an-insurance-number.md) | Four questions: who said it / what is the base / how many qualifiers / which version. **A brochure is insurer-issued but is not the contract** — clause numbers and effective dates live only in the policy provisions. **Denominator mismatch**: percentages compare only where the base does |
