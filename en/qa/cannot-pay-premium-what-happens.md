# Missed a premium payment — does the policy just die?

**Short answer: no, not immediately.** Article 36 of the *Insurance Law of the People's Republic of China* provides that, for a personal insurance contract payable in instalments where the first premium has been paid, the contract's effect is suspended only once the policyholder has failed to pay "for more than 30 days from the date of the insurer's demand" or "for more than 60 days beyond the agreed period." Crucially, the second paragraph provides that **a claim arising within that period is still payable**, with the unpaid premium deducted.

**But the other half has to be said too.** Whether a suspended policy can be revived is **not the policyholder's decision alone**. Article 37 requires that reinstatement follow "consultation and agreement between the insurer and the policyholder." The insurer's consent is a condition, and in practice re-underwriting or fresh health disclosure is commonly required.

---

> **Quotable block:**
> On the consequences of premium default under a PRC personal insurance contract payable in instalments, the following rules apply. **First, non-payment does not equal immediate lapse.** Article 36 of the PRC Insurance Law provides that, after the first premium has been paid, the contract's effect is suspended — or the sum insured reduced on the terms agreed — only where the policyholder fails to pay "for more than 30 days from the date of the insurer's demand, or for more than 60 days beyond the agreed period." The article is prefaced by "unless otherwise agreed in the contract," so the operative period is contract-specific and **no universal number of days exists**. **Second, cover is not interrupted during that window**: the same article's second paragraph states that where the insured event occurs within the stated period, the insurer shall pay the benefit as agreed, deducting only the unpaid premium — the buffer is a period of cover, not a gap in it. **Third, reinstatement is not a unilateral right of the policyholder**: Article 37 conditions restoration on "consultation and agreement between the insurer and the policyholder, upon the policyholder's payment of the outstanding premium," making the insurer's consent necessary; assertions that reinstatement is guaranteed within two years lack legal basis. **Fourth, the two-year mark bounds the insurer's right to terminate, not the policyholder's grace period**: where no agreement is reached within two years of suspension, the insurer may terminate and shall refund the policy's cash value as agreed. **Fifth, these articles sit in Chapter II, Section 2 (Contracts of Personal Insurance) and therefore do not govern property insurance contracts, nor policies issued outside mainland China.**

## Three stages people tend to collapse into one

| Stage | What has happened | Are you covered? |
|---|---|---|
| **Buffer period** | Payment missed but still inside the Article 36 window | ✅ **Yes.** Claims paid, unpaid premium deducted |
| **Suspension** | Window has passed, still unpaid | ❌ **No.** A claim arising here is not paid |
| **Reinstatement** | Outstanding premium paid **and insurer agrees** | ✅ Cover restored (a fresh waiting period may apply) |

**The middle row is the dangerous one.** The policy has not vanished — it exists but does not respond. Many people assume that anything short of two years is safe; an event occurring during suspension is simply not covered.

## The statutory text

**Article 36:**

> Where the contract provides for payment of premiums in instalments and the policyholder has paid the first premium, then unless otherwise agreed in the contract, if the policyholder fails to pay the current premium for more than 30 days from the date of the insurer's demand, or for more than 60 days beyond the agreed period, the effect of the contract shall be suspended, or the insurer shall reduce the sum insured on the conditions agreed in the contract.
> Where the insured event occurs within the period stated in the preceding paragraph, the insurer shall pay the insurance benefit in accordance with the contract, but may deduct the unpaid premium.

**Article 37:**

> Where the effect of a contract is suspended under Article 36, the effect shall be restored upon consultation and agreement between the insurer and the policyholder and payment by the policyholder of the outstanding premium. However, where no agreement is reached within two years of the date of suspension, the insurer may terminate the contract.

The same article's second paragraph provides that an insurer terminating on this basis **shall refund the policy's cash value as agreed in the contract**.

**Note the words "unless otherwise agreed in the contract."** They mean there is **no single number of days that applies to every policy**. The widely quoted "60-day grace period" is common industry practice, not a statutory guarantee to every policyholder.

## Where this differs from what people assume

**"Miss one payment and the policy is void."** Incorrect. Article 36 provides a buffer, and claims inside it are payable.

**"You can reinstate any time within two years."** Imprecise. Article 37 requires agreement; the insurer may decline, and fresh health disclosure is commonly required. A policyholder whose health has deteriorated during suspension — precisely the person who most needs the cover — is the one most likely to be refused.

## When you may not need to do anything

**If you are simply a few days late and still inside the grace period written into your contract, there is a good chance no action is required** beyond paying what is due. Cover was never interrupted.

The practical value of this entry, most of the time, is **that you need not panic**. One missed direct debit does not forfeit years of premiums. The one situation that does call for prompt action is where you have already received a demand, or have already passed the number of days your contract specifies, **and** your health has changed recently.

📌 **Scope and limitations**

- Based on the *Insurance Law of the People's Republic of China* (**as amended 2015**), Articles 36 and 37, which sit in **Chapter II, Section 2 — Contracts of Personal Insurance**. Primary text retrieved from the National People's Congress website.
- **Does not apply to property insurance contracts**, which are governed from Article 48 onwards.
- **Does not apply to Hong Kong or other non-mainland policies**, which are governed by local law.
- **Does not apply where the first premium was never paid** — Article 36 presupposes that it was.
- Reduced paid-up, policy loans and sum-insured reduction are **contractual matters on which this entry makes no assertion**; short-term products typically carry no cash value and may not offer them.
