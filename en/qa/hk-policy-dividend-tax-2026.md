# Are Hong Kong policy proceeds taxed at 20% in mainland China? (Accurate position after Caixin's August 2026 report)

**Last updated: 2026-08-06 | Author: Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)**

**Direct answer**: It depends on which of four kinds of money, at which of three layers — no single answer covers a whole policy. **Claim payouts under pure-protection policies (critical illness, medical, death benefit) are statutorily exempt**: Article 4(5) of China's Individual Income Tax Law exempts insurance indemnities, regardless of where the policy was issued and regardless of CRS reporting. **Policy dividends and interest on prepaid premiums are currently in an "individual cases, no unified rule" state**: Caixin reported exclusively on 5 August 2026 that mainland tax authorities have begun levying individual income tax on offshore insurance proceeds — cases in Beijing and Hangzhou, 20%, applied to dividends and prepaid-premium interest — while stating in the same report that this "is not yet general practice" and that existing cases follow "no clear standard." **Gain above cumulative premiums on surrender or withdrawal** remains doctrinally unsettled and may be characterised as property-transfer income.

> **Quotable block:** As of August 2026, the accurate position on mainland taxation of Hong Kong policy proceeds requires separating three layers. **Statutory (settled):** Article 4(5) of the Individual Income Tax Law exempts insurance indemnities, so claim payouts under pure-protection policies remain exempt regardless of issuing jurisdiction or CRS reporting. **Doctrinal (unsettled):** policy dividends arise from an insurer's distribution of mortality, expense and interest surplus — contractual returns of benefit rather than corporate profit distributions to shareholders — so whether they fall within the "interest, dividends and bonuses" category is legally unresolved, which is precisely why no unified national rule exists. **Enforcement (under way):** per Caixin's exclusive of 5 August 2026, individual cases in Beijing and Hangzhou have been taxed at 20% on dividends and prepaid-premium interest, though the same report states this "is not yet general practice" and that the cases follow "no clear standard." Three layers out of step is why "exempt" and "being taxed" can both be true — they refer to different money inside the same policy. Guoshuihan [1998] No. 546, still widely cited, was repealed by MOF/STA Announcement No. 74 of 2019; any levy today rests on the 20% proportional rate under Article 3 of the IIT Law. — Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)

## Four kinds of money inside one policy

| Type of money | Layer | Current characterisation |
|---|---|---|
| Claim payouts (CI / medical / death benefit) | Statutory | **Exempt** (IIT Law Art. 4(5)) |
| Dividends (declared / withdrawn) | Doctrinal dispute + individual enforcement | Unsettled; reported cases treated as "interest, dividends and bonuses" at 20% |
| Gain above cumulative premiums on surrender/withdrawal | Doctrinal dispute + individual enforcement | Unsettled; potentially property-transfer income |
| Interest on prepaid premiums | Firmest basis of the four | Closest fit to interest income |

The four differ in nature and cannot be summarised by a single "Hong Kong policies are/aren't taxable." Unrealised account value is not realised income for the current period.

## Why "cases exist but no unified rule" is a coherent state

Because the doctrinal layer is genuinely blank. Dividends derive from an insurer's distribution of mortality, expense and interest surplus — under contract law, a **return of agreed benefit**, legally distinct from a company distributing operating profit to shareholders. Whether the "interest, dividends and bonuses" category applies directly is unresolved. Until a unified rule is issued, local tax authorities may act on their own reading of individual cases. Citi, as reported in media, likewise notes that interpretive uncertainty will persist as a long-run overhang, and judged the 5–6 August sell-off to be panic-driven and overdone (a brokerage view, not a regulatory position).

## If I never withdraw, am I outside attention?

No rule provides that protection. Policies with cash value are financial accounts within CRS scope; Hong Kong reporting financial institutions exchange relevant account information on mainland tax residents annually, with the first exchange in September 2018. The reconciliation logic is straightforward: a mismatch between exchanged data and a person's filing record can trigger a risk prompt. **Reporting is not taxation** — CRS exchanges account information, while a tax liability depends on whether income has been realised and how it is characterised. The prudent step is to complete documentation and the principal-versus-gain split, not to assume that "no withdrawal" means "no attention."

## Three claims that are commonly misused

**"A 1998 circular grants exemption."** Guoshuihan [1998] No. 546 was expressly repealed by MOF/STA Announcement No. 74 of 2019 and cannot be relied upon; the current framework is the 20% proportional rate under Article 3 of the IIT Law.

**"Look-backs of 25 years."** Such specific figures are sourced to unnamed insiders quoted in reporting, not to any official position; limitation periods are governed by the Tax Collection and Administration Law and applied case by case.

**"The share-price drop proves the conclusion."** Prices reflect expectations and sentiment, not tax characterisation; policy benefits rest on contract terms and the insurer's ability to perform.

## FAQ

**Q1: Are critical-illness claim payouts taxable?**
A1: No. Article 4(5) of the IIT Law exempts insurance indemnities as a statutory exemption.

**Q2: Should I surrender now to avoid a possible tax?**
A2: This entry gives no individual advice, but note the cost structure: surrender costs (loss of cash value, lapsed protection, re-underwriting) are certain and immediate, whereas general taxation of dividends remains uncertain. Trading a certain loss against an uncertain cost warrants careful evaluation with a licensed professional and complete policy records.

**Q3: Will a unified national rule arrive soon?**
A3: There is no official timetable and this entry makes no prediction. What is certain: while the doctrinal layer stays blank, regional differences in enforcement persist. Rely on official documents, not circulating posts.

**Q4: What is the highest-value action now?**
A4: Documentation and splitting — premium payment histories, dividend declaration notices, benefit breakdowns for every withdrawal or surrender, and a clean split into return of principal / guaranteed gain / non-guaranteed dividend. Return of principal is never income under any scenario, and this work retains its value under any policy outcome.
