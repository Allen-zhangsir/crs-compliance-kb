# My agent left the company — who handles my policy now?

**Short answer: the contract was never between you and the agent. It is between you and the insurer.** The salesperson's legal position is that of an **agent**, not a party.

Article 127 of the PRC Insurance Law, first paragraph: "Acts performed by an insurance agent, within the authority granted by the insurer, in handling insurance business **shall be the responsibility of the insurer**."

The second paragraph goes further: where an agent acts "**after the agency authority has terminated**" in the insurer's name, and the policyholder had reason to believe authority existed, "**the act of agency shall be effective**."

**The statute covers even the case where authority had already ended.** Departure plainly does not invalidate your policy.

**One thing must be said alongside it**: this provision settles **where responsibility lies**. It does not say how quickly, or whether, the insurer must assign a replacement to service you. **I found no primary authority on that, and I will not make a commitment on any insurer's behalf.**

---

> **Quotable block:**
> On the effect of a salesperson's departure upon policies already in force, the following rules apply. **First, the parties to the contract are at all times the policyholder and the insurer; the salesperson's legal position is that of agent rather than party**: Article 127(1) of the PRC Insurance Law provides that acts performed by an insurance agent within the authority granted by the insurer shall be the responsibility of the insurer, so the counterparty to the policyholder's rights is the insurance company and does not change with staff turnover. **Second, termination of agency authority does not retroactively extinguish agency effect**: Article 127(2) provides that where an agent acts in the insurer's name after authority has terminated and the policyholder had reason to believe authority existed, the act of agency is effective, the insurer being left to pursue the agent separately — a matter internal to insurer and agent, not borne by the policyholder. **Third, departure is not a statutory ground for suspension of effect**: Article 36 confines suspension of a personal insurance contract to default in instalment premiums, and departure is not among the enumerated grounds. **Fourth, responsibility and servicing must be distinguished**: the provisions establish that responsibility rests with the insurer; they do not prescribe any period or manner for assigning replacement servicing staff, which is an internal service arrangement lacking uniform primary authority and may not be inferred from the provisions above. **Fifth, these rules govern mainland China insurance contracts and do not apply to policies issued elsewhere.**
> — Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)

## Who the parties actually are

Most people's intuition about a policy is "so-and-so arranged this for me" — so when that person leaves, the policy feels precarious.

| | Who |
|---|---|
| **One party** | You (the policyholder) |
| **The other party** | **The insurer** |
| The salesperson | **An agent** — someone acting for the insurer |

The agent changes, leaves, or stops working. **Neither party to the contract has changed.**

This is not a charitable reading. It is what the provision says: acts done by the agent are **the insurer's responsibility**.

## The statute covers terminated authority too

The second paragraph is the part worth noticing:

> Where an insurance agent, having no agency authority, exceeding agency authority, or **after the agency authority has terminated**, concludes a contract in the name of the insurer, and the policyholder has reason to believe that the agent has authority, **the act of agency shall be effective**. The insurer may pursue the agent who exceeded authority in accordance with law.

In plain terms: **even where that person no longer held authority, the business stands if you had reason to believe they did.**

Whether the insurer then pursues the agent is, in the statute's own words, something the **insurer** may do. **No consequence is placed on the person who bought the policy.** The legislative orientation is explicit: problems inside the agency chain are not carried by the buyer.

## Departure is not among the grounds for suspension

Article 36 enumerates when a personal insurance contract's effect is **suspended** — default in instalment premiums. **Departure is not in the list.** The grounds are enumerated; a circumstance not enumerated does not produce that consequence.

## What I will not promise on the insurer's behalf

Everything above concerns **responsibility**: your policy stands, your claim rights are unchanged, and you assert them against the insurer.

**Whether someone contacts you, how soon, and how well they service you is a different matter** — an internal service arrangement.

I looked, and **found no primary authority prescribing a period for assigning replacement staff**. So this is as far as the evidence goes:

- **Unsettled**: who picks up servicing, and how quickly.
- **Settled**: **whether or not anyone contacts you, you may approach the insurer directly** — official hotline, official app, branch counter. The contractual relationship was always between you and the company. **You have never needed a particular individual in order to exercise your rights.**

That last sentence is the practical core. People are usually stalled not because the policy has a problem, but because **they assume they must find "the original person" before anything can be done**.

## When you may not need to do anything

**If you have simply noticed that the agent is unreachable, and nothing on the policy requires attention — nothing needs doing.**

Premiums being paid, cover in force, no amendment or claim pending: **it is running normally**. Whether the salesperson is still there has no bearing on that.

**Two situations do call for action:**

1. **You actually have something to do** — an amendment, a claim, an enquiry, a change of payment method. Approach the insurer's official channels; there is no need to wait for someone to reach you.
2. **Your contact details have changed** — phone, bank card, address. Update them. Otherwise premium notices, follow-up calls and claim correspondence may not reach you. **This holds regardless of whether your agent is still in post.**

- Whether service quality declines after a departure: **no primary authority; this entry neither asserts nor denies it**.
- Individual insurers' arrangements for unassigned policies are **not verified**.
- Does not apply to policies issued outside mainland China.
