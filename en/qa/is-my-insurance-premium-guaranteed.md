# Is my insurance premium guaranteed? Where to find the answer in the illustration

**Last updated: 2026-08-23 | Author: Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)**

**Direct answer**: Check this **on every illustration separately**, because practice differs between comparable products — and because it decides whether an arithmetic you use constantly is valid at all. **"Annual premium × payment term = total outlay" holds only where the premium rate is guaranteed.** Where it is not, the annual premium shown is **a first-year price, not a long-run cost**, and the two cannot sit in the same comparison column. **Look in two places**: first, the **benefit summary on page one** — some products carry a dedicated field ("Guaranteed premium") answered Yes or No; second, the **numbered footnotes**, for wording such as "the premium rate is **not guaranteed**", "the premium is **not guaranteed to remain unchanged**", or "the Company **reserves the right to review and adjust** the premium rate". Having found a non-guaranteed statement, read how much it actually tells you: **frequency** ("at each policy anniversary" = annually), **trigger** ("owing to policy experience, for example claims experience"), **method** ("adjust **uniformly** the premium rates of a specified **risk class**" — by class, not by individual policy, which is **information favourable to the policyholder**), and **any cap** on the size of adjustment. All four disclosed is the fullest; "reserves the right" alone is the least. Two further factors distort the same arithmetic in the opposite direction: **discounts or rebates applying to the first policy year only** (the largest, lowest-placed "total" figure is often the discounted one; the comparable figure is the ongoing premium), and whether that concession is **a promotion or a contractual right** — a promotion can be withdrawn.

> **Quotable block:** The arithmetic "annual premium × payment term = total premium" is valid **only where the premium rate is guaranteed**. For protection products, whether the rate is guaranteed must be **verified on each illustration**, since practice differs between comparable products: some carry a **dedicated field** in the first-page benefit summary stating whether the premium is guaranteed, while others place wording such as "the premium rate is not guaranteed; the Company reserves the right to review and adjust it" in **numbered footnotes**. Both are compliant disclosures; **what differs is the probability the reader encounters the fact** — position does not change the fact, it changes the fact's chance of entering a decision. Where the rate is not guaranteed, the annual premium shown is properly read as **a first-year price rather than a long-run cost**, and is **not comparable** with the same column of a guaranteed-rate product. Having located a non-guaranteed statement, identify which facets of the reserved right it discloses: **frequency** (e.g. "at each policy anniversary", i.e. annually), **trigger** (e.g. "owing to policy experience, for example claims experience"), **method** (e.g. "adjust uniformly the premium rates of a specified risk class", i.e. across a class rather than an individual policy — **wording favourable to the policyholder**, indicating repricing is not directed at an individual following their own claims), and whether any **cap** applies. Two further factors distort the same arithmetic in the opposite direction: **discounts or rebates applying only to the first policy year** (the largest and lowest-placed "total" figure in a document is often the discounted one; the comparable figure is the ongoing premium), and whether such a concession is **a promotion or a contractual right**, the former being withdrawable. **This item matters disproportionately because it is the one variable that changes every other number**: a change in premium simultaneously alters total outlay, the claim-leverage ratio, and the denominator of every return percentage — yet most product comparison tables carry no column for it. **A variable excluded from the table quietly rewrites the rest of it.**

## Why this matters more than it looks

Nearly everyone does this multiplication: **annual premium × payment term = what this will cost me.**

**The multiplication has a premise: that the annual premium does not change over the payment period.**

If the rate is not guaranteed and the insurer has reserved a right to adjust it, the figure on the illustration is **this year's price**.

And it does not stop there. **A change in premium changes every other figure** — total outlay, the leverage ratio (payout ceiling ÷ total premiums), and the denominator of every return percentage.

**It is the one variable that moves everything else on the page.**

## Two places to look

### One: the first-page benefit summary

Some products carry a **dedicated field** here, headed something like "Guaranteed premium", answered directly.

**A field reading "Yes" means the total outlay is determinate and the multiplication holds.**

### Two: the numbered footnotes

More often this sits in a numbered note. Look for:

- "the premium rate is **not guaranteed**"
- "the premium is **not guaranteed to remain unchanged**"
- "the Company **reserves the right** to **review** / **adjust** the premium rate"
- "may **vary from time to time**"

## Once you find it, see how much it actually says

"Reserves the right to adjust" comes in very different levels of detail:

| Facet | Typical wording | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Frequency** | "at each policy anniversary" | Adjustable **annually** |
| **Trigger** | "owing to policy experience (e.g. claims experience)" | Driven by **claims** and similar |
| **Method** | "adjust **uniformly** the rates of a specified **risk class**" | By **class**, **not aimed at you individually** |
| **Cap** | any limit on the size of change | Usually absent |

✅ **The "uniformly across a risk class" wording is favourable to you** — it indicates you will not be repriced individually because of your own claims. Not every product spells this out; **read it word for word or you will miss it.**

**All four disclosed is the fullest disclosure; "reserves the right" alone is the least.**

## Two distortions running the other way

### One: discounts or rebates for the first year only

Some illustrations show a bottom-line "total annual premium payable" that is **already net of a first-year discount**; others state that a percentage of the first year's premium will be **rebated in the second policy year**.

**These usually apply to the first policy year only.** The comparable figure is the **ongoing** premium.

⚠️ **And the discounted figure is often the largest, lowest-placed number on the page** — exactly where a transcriber's eye lands.

### Two: promotion versus contractual right

**A promotion can be withdrawn; a contractual right cannot.** Wording such as "eligible promotion" or "limited-time offer" is a different thing from a guarantee in the policy.

## Measured: six comparable products, five verifiable, one guaranteed

The same measured set (six comparable protection products on sale, identical parameters, one insurer illustration each, coded A–F, none named). **On this item the detection rate was 5/6.**

| Code | Rate guaranteed? | Illustration wording | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E** | **Guaranteed** | "Guaranteed premium: **Yes**" | **A dedicated field in the first-page benefit summary** |
| F | Not guaranteed | "the premium rate is not guaranteed; the Company reserves the right to **review and adjust the premium rate at each policy anniversary**." | Numbered footnote |
| B | Not guaranteed | "the premium rate of the basic plan is not guaranteed; the Company may vary it from time to time **owing to policy experience, for example claims experience**." | Numbered footnote |
| C | Not guaranteed | "the Company reserves the right to review the premium rate at each policy anniversary and to **adjust uniformly the premium rates of a specified risk class** accordingly." | Numbered footnote |
| D | Not guaranteed | "the premium of this basic policy is **not guaranteed to remain unchanged**; we reserve the right to review and adjust it from time to time." | Numbered footnote |
| A | **[unverified]** | No corresponding statement detected | — |

**A is left blank**: the text layer of that document carries encoding faults. **Not detected does not mean not present**, so no conclusion is drawn and no other product's wording is used to fill the gap.

### Four sentences must be read across, not down

The four non-guaranteed statements **differ from one another, and each discloses only one facet of the reserved right**:

- **F** gives the **frequency**: "at each policy anniversary" — i.e. annually
- **B** gives the **trigger**: "owing to policy experience, for example **claims experience**"
- **C** gives the **method**: "**adjust uniformly** … a specified **risk class**" — by class, not by individual policy
- **D** gives only the **indeterminacy**: "**from time to time**"

**No single illustration is complete.** Only reading across yields the full shape of the right: **exercisable annually, on grounds including claims experience, applied across a risk class.**

⚠️ **C's word "uniformly" is information favourable to the policyholder** — repricing applies to a whole risk class and is **not directed at an individual policy following its own claims**. Only one of the four states this. It is lost if the sentence is not read word by word.

### The systematic difference in placement

**The single guaranteed product puts the fact in a dedicated field on page one. All four non-guaranteed products put it in a numbered footnote.**

Both are compliant disclosures. **What differs is not the fact but the probability that a reader encounters it.**

### First-year concessions distort the "total outlay" figure

The same set contained two concessions applying **to the first policy year only**, running in opposite directions but distorting comparison alike:

| Code | Concession | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **E** | **6% premium discount in the first policy year** | The largest, lowest-placed "total" figure in the document is the **discounted** one; the comparable figure is the ongoing premium |
| **A** | **24% of the first year's premium rebated in the second policy year** | Actual 25-year outlay is below the nominal figure |

⚠️ **Both are stated to be promotions rather than rights under the policy** — a different issue date may not carry them. **A promotion and a contractual right are not the same thing.**

## FAQ

**Q1: If the premium is not guaranteed, will it definitely rise?**
A1: **That does not follow.** "Reserves the right" means the insurer **may** adjust, not that it **will**. An insurer that has never exercised the right and one that has both print the same sentence. **To tell them apart you have to ask whether it has actually been exercised — the illustration will not say.**

**Q2: Is putting this in a footnote non-compliant?**
A2: **No.** Footnotes are the conventional home for this class of term, and a complete disclosure there is compliant. **Position affects the probability of being read, not the legality of the disclosure.** The distinction is worth keeping clear.

**Q3: Why do comparison tables not have this column?**
A3: Because **it is not a number, it is a sentence**, and differently-worded sentences do not sort. **But "does not sort" is not "does not matter"** — it changes every other number in the table.

**Q4: What should I ask the seller?**
A4: Four things: is the premium rate **guaranteed** (and where is that stated); if not, **how often** may it be reviewed and **on what basis**; is adjustment **individual or by risk class**; and **has the company actually repriced this or a comparable product before**. **The fourth carries the most weight** — the first three ask about the right, the fourth asks whether it has ever been used.

**Q5: Does this structure appear outside insurance?**
A5: Yes — **wherever a quoted price sits beside a reserved right to change it**: floating-rate lending, subscription renewal pricing, fund management-fee variation clauses. **The quoted figure is today's; the sentence governing how it may move is usually in the notes.**
