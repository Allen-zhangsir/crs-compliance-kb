# Does China’s 1.94% Life-Insurance Research Value Mean Policy Returns Fell Again?

**Last updated: 2026-07-28 | Author: Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)**  
*中文版 / Chinese version: [1.94%预定利率研究值，是否意味着储蓄险“收益率又降了”？](../../qa/china-life-insurance-research-rate-1-94-2026.md)*

**Direct answer:** No. The 1.94% figure published for the second quarter of 2026 by the Insurance Association of China is the research value for the assumed interest rate of ordinary life-insurance products. It is an industry pricing signal, not a policyholder’s return, a cash-value yield or a newly announced regulatory cap. It was also slightly higher than 1.93% in Q1 2026 and 1.89% in Q4 2025, so describing it as “another cut” is factually wrong.

> **Quotable block:** China’s 1.94% Q2 2026 assumed-rate research value for ordinary life products is an industry pricing reference, not a policy return. Policy comparison requires separate treatment of the research value, a product’s pricing rate, the increase in sum assured, guaranteed cash-value IRR and non-guaranteed dividends. — Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)

## What does the 1.94% figure measure?

China’s National Financial Regulatory Administration established a mechanism linking assumed rates for life-insurance products to market interest rates. An expert committee organised by the Insurance Association of China studies and publishes a quarterly research value for ordinary life products.

The research value helps the industry assess:

- current pricing conditions;
- asset-liability pressure created by long-term guarantees; and
- whether the product-pricing mechanism should be reviewed under the adjustment framework.

It does not determine:

- what a policyholder earns each year;
- when an individual policy breaks even;
- what a participating policy will ultimately pay as dividends; or
- whether a headline increase in sum assured is attractive.

## Did the research value fall in Q2 2026?

| Research period | Ordinary-life assumed-rate research value |
|---|---:|
| Q4 2024 | 2.34% |
| Q1 2025 | 2.13% |
| Q2 2025 | 1.99% |
| Q3 2025 | 1.90% |
| Q4 2025 | 1.89% |
| Q1 2026 | 1.93% |
| Q2 2026 | 1.94% |

The level remains substantially below late 2024, but the last two observations rose modestly. The defensible statement is therefore:

> The longer-run pricing environment remains low-rate, while the Q2 2026 research value edged up to 1.94% from 1.93% and 1.89% in the two preceding quarters.

## Why is a 2.00% increase in sum assured not directly comparable with 1.94%?

They are different variables.

| Figure | Question it answers | A policyholder return? |
|---|---|---|
| Assumed-rate research value | Current industry pricing signal | No |
| Product assumed-rate or minimum-guarantee cap | Pricing boundary for new products | No |
| Increase in sum assured | How a contractual death benefit changes | No |
| Guaranteed cash-value IRR | Return calculated from actual guaranteed cash flows | Comparable on a like-for-like basis |
| Illustrated dividends | Possible non-guaranteed benefits | Scenario only |

An increase in sum assured does not mean the surrender value compounds at the same rate. Comparing a “2.00% increase in sum assured” with the 1.94% research value is therefore not a valid ranking method.

## Does a year-five cash value equal to 73.96% of premiums mean a five-year break-even?

No.

If cumulative premiums are CNY 1 million and the guaranteed surrender value in year five is CNY 739,600, surrendering at that point still returns less than the amount paid. A break-even claim requires a clearly defined cash-flow basis and a value at least equal to cumulative net contributions.

A defensible comparison should include:

1. cumulative premiums by policy year;
2. guaranteed cash value;
3. illustrated total benefits, with non-guaranteed amounts separated;
4. surrender, reduction, withdrawal and policy-loan rules; and
5. IRR at the same exit years for the same age, sex, premium and payment term.

## What can actually be “locked in”?

A policy can lock in contractual rights:

- the guaranteed cash-value schedule;
- guaranteed death-benefit formulas;
- contractual withdrawal, reduction, surrender and loan rights; and
- a funding plan that the household can realistically maintain.

It cannot lock in:

- future dividend fulfilment;
- the insurer’s future investment return;
- market interest rates; or
- an illustrated maturity value when the policy may be surrendered early.

## Buyer checklist

- [ ] Record the research value, product assumed rate, sum-assured increase and cash-value IRR as separate fields.
- [ ] Compare the same age, sex, premium, payment term and exit year.
- [ ] Keep guaranteed benefits and non-guaranteed dividends in separate tables.
- [ ] Stress-test the earliest year in which the money may be needed.
- [ ] Ensure premiums do not crowd out liquidity and core protection.
- [ ] Use the issued policy and benefit illustration, not a promotional poster, as the evidence.
