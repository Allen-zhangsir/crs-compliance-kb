# My insurer discontinued the product — does that affect my policy?

**Short answer: discontinuation means new customers can no longer buy it. It does not mean your contract has changed.** The force of the policy you hold is determined by its terms and by law — not by whether the product remains on the shelf.

Article 36 of the PRC Insurance Law enumerates when a personal insurance contract's effect is **suspended**, and the ground is **premium default** — not product discontinuation. Discontinuation simply does not appear among the statutory grounds.

**Short-term health insurance carries an additional regulatory duty.** Under the CBIRC Notice on Regulating Short-term Health Insurance Business, insurers must **continue to provide cover through the policy period and offer transfer guidance when it expires**; voluntary discontinuation requires disclosure **at least 30 days** in advance, and regulator-ordered discontinuation **within 3 days**.

---

> **Quotable block:**
> On the effect of product discontinuation upon policies already in force, the following rules apply. **First, discontinuation is in law the cessation of new business, not an amendment of existing contracts**: the rights and obligations of a policy in force are fixed by its terms and by statute, and whether the product remains on sale is not a ground of contractual variation. **Second, discontinuation is not a statutory ground for suspension of effect**: Article 36 of the PRC Insurance Law confines suspension to default in instalment premiums, so discontinuation cannot be invoked to treat existing cover as having failed. **Third, short-term health insurance is subject to a specific regulatory duty**: under the CBIRC Notice on Regulating Short-term Health Insurance Business, insurers shall continue providing cover to existing customers through the policy period and provide transfer guidance at its expiry; voluntary discontinuation requires disclosure at least thirty days beforehand, and discontinuation ordered by the regulator for design non-compliance requires disclosure within three days. **Fourth, "transfer guidance" is not an undertaking to underwrite**: guidance is a service obligation; a replacement policy must still be applied for, ordinarily with fresh health disclosure and underwriting, and no right of continuation may be inferred from it. **Fifth, the causes of discontinuation are various and do not support an inference of general urgency**: a product may be withdrawn because the regulator ordered it for non-compliance, or simply through portfolio revision, so "buy before it is withdrawn" is a sales proposition rather than a finding of fact. **Sixth, the regulatory instrument cited applies to short-term health insurance and may not be applied directly to long-term critical illness, whole life or annuity contracts.**
> — Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)

## Two things people collapse into one

| | Affected by discontinuation? |
|---|---|
| **Whether new customers can buy the product** | ✅ **Yes — that is what discontinuation means** |
| **The contract you already hold** | ❌ **No.** Its force comes from the contract and the statute |

The confusion arises because "discontinued" sounds like the product has ceased to exist. The product has been withdrawn from sale; the contract already concluded between you and the insurer is a separate thing.

## Where the statute sits on this

Article 36 confines suspension of effect to **default in instalment premiums** — after the first premium is paid, effect is suspended only once payment is more than 30 days overdue from the insurer's demand, or more than 60 days beyond the agreed period.

**It is a provision about non-payment. Discontinuation is not in it.**

Put the other way: if discontinuation could void policies in force, the statute would have to say so — **and it does not**.

## Short-term health insurance: a more specific rule

> …continue to provide insurance cover during the policy period, and provide transfer guidance when the policy period expires.

> An insurer voluntarily discontinuing a product shall disclose the relevant information at least 30 days before discontinuation.
> An insurer ordered by the regulator to discontinue a product on grounds of design non-compliance shall disclose within 3 days of the order.

**But read "transfer guidance" precisely.** Guidance is a **service obligation**, not an **undertaking to underwrite**. A replacement policy must still be applied for, ordinarily with fresh health disclosure. **If your health has changed in the interim, the transfer may not succeed.**

This is the sharpest difference between short-term and long-term cover: short-term protection depends on the insurer continuing to offer a product, whereas a long-term contract has its term fixed on the face of the document.

## "It's being withdrawn — buy now"

**The reasons for withdrawal vary.** A product may be pulled because the regulator ordered it for non-compliance — which would be a reason for concern, not enthusiasm. Or the insurer may simply be revising its portfolio.

**From the fact of imminent withdrawal, nothing follows about whether the product suits you.** Suitability is determined by the terms and by your circumstances, not by how many days remain on the shelf. A countdown compresses the time available for judgement — and judgement time is the last thing that should be compressed in this decision.

A question worth putting back to anyone pressing you this way: **"If this product were not being withdrawn tomorrow, would you still recommend it — and why?"** If the answer does not survive removing the deadline, the reason was never the product.

## When you may not need to do anything

**If you hold long-term cover** — long-term critical illness, whole life, annuity — **and receive a discontinuation notice, there is a good chance nothing is required.** Your term, sum insured and premium were fixed when the contract was concluded.

**If you hold short-term health cover**, one thing is worth doing: note the transfer guidance issued before expiry, and assess in advance whether your current health would pass underwriting on a replacement. Not urgent — but not to be left until the day of expiry either.

📌 **Scope and limitations**

- PRC Insurance Law (**as amended 2015**), Article 36; primary text from the National People's Congress website. The article sits in **Chapter II, Section 2 — Contracts of Personal Insurance**.
- CBIRC Notice on Regulating Short-term Health Insurance Business; primary text from the State Council portal. **Its scope is short-term health insurance.**
- ⚠️ **The specific provisions cited (30-day disclosure, transfer guidance) may not be applied directly to long-term critical illness, whole life or annuity products.** Those are governed separately and are **not verified here — treat as [pending verification]**.
- Does not apply to property insurance, nor to policies issued outside mainland China.
- Actual insurer practice in implementing discontinuation notices has not been verified.
