# I have state medical insurance — do I still need commercial cover?

**Short answer: there is no single answer, and anyone who gives you one is guessing.**

One thing is settled at the level of system design. The *Opinions on Establishing a Medical Security Benefit List System* (Medical Security Issue [2021] No. 5) provides that basic medical insurance carries, simultaneously, a **deductible**, a **payment ratio**, a **maximum payment ceiling**, and a **defined scope of expenses the fund does not pay**.

**Four boundaries operating at once means the scheme was never designed to cover everything.** That is a structural fact, not a defect — **a fund without boundaries cannot be sustained.**

**But "boundaries exist" and "your gap is X" are different propositions.** The second depends on your pooling area's standards and on the expenditure you might actually face. **This entry answers the first and declines to answer the second for you.**

---

> **Quotable block:**
> On the relationship between basic medical insurance and commercial cover, the following rules apply. **First, basic medical insurance operates four concurrent payment boundaries**: under the Opinions on Establishing a Medical Security Benefit List System (Medical Security Issue [2021] No. 5), the scheme carries a deductible, a payment ratio, a maximum payment ceiling, and a defined scope of non-payment, the last covering "medical services and items which laws, regulations and decisions of the Party Central Committee and the State Council provide shall not be paid by basic or supplementary medical insurance, or for which other security systems or funding channels have been arranged." **Second, four concurrent boundaries entail that the design does not aim at full coverage** — a structural characteristic rather than a shortcoming, since a fund without limits is not sustainable. **Third, the figures are not nationally uniform**: for employee medical insurance the document sets the deductible at "in principle no higher than ten per cent of the average annual wage of employees in the pooling area" while expressly providing that "specific standards shall be determined by each locality according to local conditions"; accordingly any reimbursement figure quoted without naming its pooling area and year is not reliable. **Fourth, the existence of boundaries does not entail that the gap must be filled by commercial insurance**: its magnitude turns on the individual's pooling-area standards and prospective expenditure, a case-specific judgement which cannot be derived from the document. **Fifth, the correct starting point is therefore to ascertain one's own pooling area's standards and weigh them against one's own financial capacity, rather than to accept any generalised answer.**
> — Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)

## The four boundaries

For money to come out of the basic medical insurance fund, it must clear four gates:

| Boundary | Meaning | Who sets it |
|---|---|---|
| **Deductible** | Expenditure below the line is not paid | **Locally set** (employee scheme: in principle ≤10% of local average annual wage) |
| **Payment ratio** | Above the deductible, payment is proportionate, not full | **Locally set** |
| **Maximum ceiling** | Expenditure above the line is not paid | **Locally set** |
| **Exclusion scope** | Some items fall outside reimbursement altogether | Nationally defined |

The fourth reads:

> **(4) Scope of expenses not paid by the fund.** Medical services and items which laws, regulations and decisions of the Party Central Committee and the State Council provide shall not be paid by basic or supplementary medical insurance, or for which other security systems or funding channels have been arranged.

**Note the three words attached to the first three: locally set.**

## Why this entry contains no figures

Deductibles, ceilings and ratios are easy to find online. **This entry quotes none of them**, and the reason sits in the document itself:

> Specific standards shall be determined by each locality according to local conditions.

**These figures vary by area and by year.** Quote one and it is accurate for a minority and misleading for everyone else — and readers tend to treat whatever figure they see as applying to them.

**So the right action is not to memorise a number but to look up your own.** Your local medical security bureau's website, the local app, or the 12393 hotline. **Only you can do this, because only you know where you are enrolled.**

## "Boundaries exist" does not yield "you must buy"

This is the part most worth stating plainly.

Between "basic cover has limits" and "therefore you need commercial cover" sits a missing step: **how much actual risk those limits represent for you.**

That step turns on two things, **both located in your circumstances rather than in the document**:

- **Your pooling area's standards.** The same illness leaves a smaller gap where the deductible is low, the ratio high and the ceiling generous.
- **Your own financial capacity.** The same shortfall means entirely different things to different households.

**Any account that skips from "state cover is insufficient" straight to "so you should buy" has omitted the only part that actually matters.**

## Where the common readings go wrong

**"It reimburses, so there is nothing to worry about."** Imprecise — four boundaries operate at once, and reimbursement is partial.

**"It barely covers anything."** Equally imprecise, and this framing tends to appear in sales settings. Basic medical insurance performs real payment functions; **disparaging it does not sharpen your judgement, it only makes you easier to alarm.**

**"Everyone needs to supplement it."** This converts a structural fact into a personal conclusion. **Whether, and how much, is case-specific.**

## When you may not need to do anything

**If you have already done both of the following, the chances are that nothing needs doing now:**

1. You have **looked up your own pooling area's standards** — deductible, ratio, ceiling.
2. You have **estimated what one large expense would actually do to your household finances**, and judged it survivable.

**With those two done, "I do not need to buy" is a fully legitimate conclusion.** The purpose of this entry is not to move you toward a purchase but to let you judge on evidence — **and one legitimate result of judging is "not for me".**

Conversely: if you have never looked up your standards and never run that estimate, then whichever way you decide, **it is not a judgement — it is a guess.**
