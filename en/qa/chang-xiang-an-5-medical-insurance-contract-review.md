# How Should “No Health Questionnaire,” “Zero Deductible,” and Nodule Cover Be Read in Chang Xiang An 5?

**Last updated: 2026-07-28 | Author: Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)**  
*中文版 / Chinese version: [长相安5号的“免健告、0免赔、高危结节可赔”，应该怎样读？](../../qa/chang-xiang-an-5-medical-insurance-contract-review.md)*

**Direct answer:** Read each claim through the contract. The marketing phrases do not mean that every pre-existing condition is covered, every yuan is reimbursed in full, or renewal is guaranteed. The public disclosure describes Chang Xiang An 5 as a one-year mainland Chinese medical policy with non-guaranteed renewal. General medical expenses within the CNY 20,000 or 30,000 threshold are reimbursed at 60%, while the high-risk-nodule benefit has a 90-day waiting period, CNY 30,000 annual deductible, initial 30% reimbursement rate and CNY 10,000 annual cap.

> **Quotable block:** “No health questionnaire” primarily improves access; it does not make every pre-existing condition payable. “Zero deductible” can still sit alongside a 60% reimbursement rate. High-risk-nodule cover is a narrow benefit with its own wait, deductible, percentage and cap. — Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)

## What is the product?

According to the public product disclosure:

- the insurer is PICC Property and Casualty Company Limited;
- the product is a mainland Chinese commercial medical policy;
- the insurance period is one year;
- renewal is not guaranteed and requires a new application accepted by the insurer; and
- territory, hospital network, drug access and version-specific terms remain subject to the issued documents.

Access today and renewal next year are separate questions.

## What does “no health questionnaire” solve?

Removing a conventional health questionnaire can reduce the entry barrier. It does not establish that:

- all pre-existing conditions are covered;
- severe prior diseases have no exclusion;
- every nodule falls within the nodule benefit;
- renewal is guaranteed; or
- medical history will not be reviewed at claim.

The public disclosure still contains exclusions for specified severe pre-existing conditions and separate benefit boundaries.

## Why does “zero deductible” not mean zero out-of-pocket cost?

The disclosed general-medical formula is tiered:

| Scenario | Disclosed reimbursement summary |
|---|---|
| Within the CNY 20,000/30,000 threshold | 60% |
| Above the threshold | 100% |
| Insured on a social-insurance basis but not settled through social insurance | Relevant percentages may fall to 36%/60% |

Even where one layer is described as having no deductible, the claim can still be affected by:

- a 60% reimbursement rate;
- social-insurance settlement;
- hospital and department restrictions;
- waiting periods;
- exclusions; and
- annual limits or drug conditions.

The actual out-of-pocket amount must therefore be calculated from the full formula.

## How broad is the high-risk-nodule benefit?

The public page describes a separate, narrow benefit rather than a simple extension of general medical cover:

- 90-day waiting period;
- CNY 30,000 annual deductible;
- initial 30% reimbursement rate;
- a possible increase to 50% after qualifying continuous, claim-free reapplications; and
- CNY 10,000 annual maximum.

The hospital scope is also subject to the contract, including specified public-hospital ordinary departments.

The accurate phrase is not merely “high-risk nodules are covered”; the four contractual numbers must travel with that statement.

## Can some first-year procedures face an additional reduction?

Yes, according to the public disclosure.

In a first or non-continuous policy year, listed procedures involving benign tumours, tuberculosis, polyps, hyperplasia, cysts, nodules, stones, haemorrhoids or fistulas can be subject to a further 60% multiplier.

If the underlying reimbursement rate is 60%, applying a further 60% multiplier can produce an effective 36% rate before other social-insurance, hospital, deductible and limit rules.

## Does “external drugs without a fixed list” mean every drug is covered?

No.

Even where a static brand-name list is not used, a drug can still need to satisfy:

- the defined insured benefit;
- approved indication;
- marketing-approval date or contract scope;
- prescription and designated-channel rules;
- insurer review or prior authorisation; and
- exclusions and updated network requirements.

“Not limited to one static list” is not the same as “no drug restrictions”.

## Does the mid-to-high-end label mean all VIP and private departments are covered?

No.

The public disclosure indicates that:

- special or VIP access for general medical care generally depends on an optional benefit and may carry a CNY 20,000 deductible;
- specified critical illness may extend to listed special or VIP departments, depending on the benefit;
- the high-risk-nodule benefit does not automatically expand to every special department; and
- hospital and department lists remain version-specific.

## Who may find the product useful?

Potential fit:

- people who struggle to pass a conventional health questionnaire;
- people who accept a one-year, non-guaranteed-renewal structure;
- people who have checked that their main condition is not within the severe pre-existing-condition exclusion; and
- people using the policy as a transitional or supplementary layer.

Particular caution is warranted for people who:

- need guaranteed long-term renewal;
- expect their main future claim to arise from an excluded severe condition;
- interpret “zero deductible” as full first-dollar reimbursement; or
- depend on unrestricted private, VIP, overseas or external-drug access.

## Seven-item pre-purchase checklist

- [ ] Issued wording and product description
- [ ] One-year and non-guaranteed-renewal language
- [ ] Severe pre-existing-condition exclusion
- [ ] Correct CNY 20,000/30,000 threshold version
- [ ] Reimbursement rates with and without social-insurance settlement
- [ ] Nodule wait, deductible, rate and annual cap
- [ ] Hospital, department, drug and external-pharmacy lists
