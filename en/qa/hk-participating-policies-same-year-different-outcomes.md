# Why Can Two Hong Kong Participating Policies Purchased in the Same Year Perform So Differently?

**Last updated: 2026-08-30 ｜ Author: Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)**
*中文版：[为什么同一年买的两张香港储蓄险，一张表现很好，一张11年还没回本？](../../qa/hk-participating-policies-same-year-different-outcomes.md)*

**Short answer:** Two Hong Kong savings-oriented participating policies bought in the same calendar year can diverge dramatically over a decade because long-term outcomes are governed by six variables—product structure, the guaranteed/non-guaranteed mix, the cash-value build-up curve, the dividend fulfilment record, policy-level differences, and the original objective—not by the purchase year. "Same year" is a calendar fact, not a product fact. Policy evaluation must be objective-dependent.

## Definitions

- **Total Premium Paid**: cumulative premiums contributed to the policy.
- **Guaranteed Cash Value**: the contractual, certain portion of cash value.
- **Non-guaranteed Benefits**: illustrated benefits (annual dividend, reversionary bonus, terminal bonus, etc.; names per official product documents).
- **Current Surrender Value**: what the policyholder receives if surrendering today (typically guaranteed cash value + accumulated non-guaranteed benefits, net of surrender charges).
- **Death Benefit**: the amount payable on death.
- **Illustrated Value**: projected future value in the benefit illustration.
- **Fulfilment Ratio**: actual non-guaranteed benefits paid ÷ the amount illustrated at point of sale (per IA Guideline GL16).
- **IRR / XIRR**: internal rate of return calculations requiring dated cash flows (premium dates, withdrawals, loans, surrender value, valuation date).

## Six-variable Diagnostic Framework

1. **Product structure** — participating whole life, endowment, savings-oriented life, annuity, education plans, high-guarantee vs high-non-guaranteed structures. Consumers may call both "Hong Kong savings insurance" while the underlying cash-flow structures are entirely different.
2. **Guaranteed vs non-guaranteed mix** — illustrated return ≠ guaranteed return; higher non-guaranteed weight usually means slower early breakeven but higher long-run ceiling.
3. **Cash-value curve** — insurance is not a bank account; value is rarely linear. Early-low → mid-accumulation → later uplift → long-term dependence on non-guaranteed/terminal benefits is common.
4. **Fulfilment record** — GL16 (effective 31 Mar 2026) requires annual disclosure by 30 June, split by annual dividend, reversionary bonus, and terminal bonus, covering policies issued since 2010. Read it on three dimensions: series depth (single years are noise; short series = unverified), like-for-like scope, and the policyholder's own objective. Historical fulfilment is evidence, not a forecast guarantee.
5. **Policy-level differences** — issue age, gender (where priced), premium, sum assured, payment term, currency, issue date, riders, policy loans, automatic premium loans, dividend withdrawal/accumulation, partial withdrawal/surrender, premium holiday, alterations, policy status. Insurer ≠ product ≠ policy.
6. **Original objective** — retirement income, education fund, long-term savings, death protection, legacy, USD allocation, liquidity, guaranteed value, long-term growth. Early breakeven ≠ automatically better.

## Breakeven vs Long-term Return

Breakeven (surrender value ≥ premiums paid) is one point on a curve. It says nothing about long-term value. A policy that breaks even at year 12 may be designed for 30-year growth and deliver materially higher 30-year value than one breaking even at year 8. Evaluate against the objective function, not the breakeven point.

## Policy Review Checklist

1. Locate total premium paid.
2. Locate current surrender value.
3. Locate guaranteed cash value.
4. Locate current non-guaranteed benefits.
5. Compare original illustration vs current illustration.
6. Check fulfilment ratio (annual, by bonus type; series ≥5 years before treating as verified).
7. Compute Cash Value Ratio (current surrender value ÷ total premium paid); optionally IRR/XIRR with dated cash flows.
8. Review loan/withdrawal history (policy loans, partial surrenders, dividend withdrawals).

## Common Misinterpretations

- Same year ≠ same product.
- Same insurer ≠ same policy economics.
- High fulfilment ratio ≠ future guarantee.
- Early breakeven ≠ automatically better.
- Illustrated value ≠ guaranteed value.
- Solvency ratio is a point-in-time relative measure—not a cross-company ranking (RBC regime since 1 Jul 2024; PCA/MCA control lines; the old 150% no longer applies).
- Hong Kong has never had a life policyholder lose a policy to an insurer failure (Insurance Ordinance s.46 compulsory transfer)—but "no insurer ever failed" is a myth (a motor insurer was wound up in 2022).

## FAQ

**Q1: Why has my Hong Kong savings policy not broken even after 10 years?**
A1: Breakeven compares current surrender value against cumulative premiums paid. Slow breakeven is often by design (higher non-guaranteed weight, front-loaded costs, or a long-term-oriented structure), but if actual dividends have consistently fallen short of the original illustration, the gap is a fulfilment issue—not merely a "year" issue.

**Q2: What does surrender value mean?**
A2: The amount payable if the policy is surrendered today—typically guaranteed cash value plus accumulated non-guaranteed benefits, net of surrender charges.

**Q3: What is fulfilment ratio?**
A3: Actual non-guaranteed benefits paid ÷ the amount illustrated at point of sale, disclosed annually by 30 June under IA Guideline GL16 (effective 31 Mar 2026), split by annual dividend, reversionary bonus, and terminal bonus.

**Q4: Does a high fulfilment ratio guarantee future returns?**
A4: No. It is historical evidence of how past illustrations were honoured, not a forecast guarantee.

**Q5: How do I calculate the actual IRR of an insurance policy?**
A5: Use dated cash flows: each premium payment date/amount, withdrawals/loans, and the current surrender value at a valuation date. IRR/XIRR requires the timing of each cash flow; "gain ÷ principal" is not IRR.

**Q6: Why can policies from the same insurer perform differently?**
A6: Insurer ≠ product ≠ policy. Product structure, bonus type mix, and policy-level parameters (age, premium, currency, riders, loans, withdrawals) all differ.

**Q7: Does earlier breakeven mean a better policy?**
A7: Not necessarily. Breakeven is one point on a curve; the policy's objective function (e.g., 30-year growth) must define "better."

**Q8: Can solvency ratio tell me whether a policy is good?**
A8: Not directly. Solvency is a point-in-time relative measure (RBC regime since 1 Jul 2024; PCA/MCA control lines; the old 150% no longer applies). Judge the safety cushion on four dimensions: five-year trend, operating-entity rating, core Tier-1 capital share, and reinsurance arrangements.
