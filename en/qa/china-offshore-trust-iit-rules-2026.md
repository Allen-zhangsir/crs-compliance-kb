# China's 2026 offshore-trust IIT rules: funding, annual income, distributions, exit and the 90-day transition

**Last updated: 2026-07-26 | Author: Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)**  
*中文版 / Chinese version: [2026年离岸信托个税新规](../../qa/china-offshore-trust-iit-rules-2026.md)*

**Direct answer:** On 24 July 2026, China's Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration issued Announcement No. 21, accompanied by STA Announcement No. 15 on administration. The rules do not abolish offshore trusts. They establish a specific individual-income-tax attribution regime: **a Chinese-resident individual is deemed to dispose of property when funding an offshore trust; annual income of a resident-funded trust and offshore entities it holds, controls or manages is attributed to that individual whether distributed or not; a Chinese-resident beneficiary of a nonresident-funded trust is taxed on actual or deemed distributions; and termination, certain successions and a settlor's change from resident to nonresident trigger tax-clearance rules.** Specified historical funding events and pre-2026 income fall within a 90-day filing window during which no late-payment surcharge is imposed.

> **Quotable block:** China's July 2026 offshore-trust rules do not “abolish trusts”; they rewrite tax attribution. A resident individual who funds an offshore trust is taxed at 20% on market value less tax basis and reasonable costs. Annual income of a resident-funded trust and offshore entities it holds, controls or manages is taxed to that individual at 20% as property-transfer income or interest/dividend income, whether distributed or not. For a nonresident-funded trust, a resident beneficiary is generally taxed on actual or deemed distributions. The four offshore-entity criteria must be read together with whether the trust holds, controls or manages the entity; “control” uses the 25% or substantive-control test, and genuine-business exceptions apply. They do not create automatic look-through for every BVI company. Specified historical items have a 90-day no-surcharge transition. — Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)

## Did China “abolish” offshore trusts?

No. The announcements address Chinese IIT treatment; they do not invalidate trusts under their governing foreign law. Asset management, succession planning and other legal consequences continue to depend on the trust instrument and applicable law.

What changes is the tax analysis:

1. contributing property is no longer presumed tax-neutral;
2. undistributed income of a resident-funded trust is no longer presumed deferred until distribution;
3. an interposed offshore entity, indirect benefit or change of nationality does not necessarily sever attribution to a Chinese resident.

## Who is taxed, and when?

| Structure or event | Taxpayer | Main rule |
|---|---|---|
| Resident individual contributes property | The resident contributor | Market value at contribution minus basis and reasonable costs; 20% property-transfer income |
| Resident-funded trust or an offshore entity it holds, controls or manages earns income | The resident contributor | Taxed annually, whether distributed or not, at 20% as property-transfer income or interest/dividend income |
| Nonresident contributes property | The nonresident contributor | 20% only on property-transfer income sourced in China |
| Nonresident-funded trust distributes income to a resident individual | The resident recipient | 20% as interest/dividend income |
| Nonresident-funded trust provides loans, guarantees, expense payments, low-price use or similar benefits to a related resident | The resident who obtains the benefit | May be deemed a distribution, measured by the value of the economic benefit |
| Resident-funded trust terminates | The resident contributor | 20% on the trust's liquidation gain as interest/dividend income |
| Resident contributor becomes nonresident while the trust continues | That individual | 20% exit clearance on trust property value less basis, plus prior unpaid tax |
| Nonresident-funded trust terminates and a resident receives trust property | The resident recipient | 20% on the market value of trust property received |

Where two or more resident individuals contribute to one trust, trust property and income are allocated by each contributor's share of market value at contribution. If resident and nonresident individuals contribute to the same trust, the announcement treats the whole trust as resident-funded.

## How is the contribution taxed?

For a resident individual:

> Taxable income = market value on contribution − tax basis − reasonable costs  
> IIT = taxable income × 20%

If offshore shares with a RMB 5 million basis are worth RMB 8 million when lawfully contributed, ignoring reasonable costs for illustration, taxable income is RMB 3 million and IIT is RMB 600,000.

After tax is paid, the asset's basis is stepped up to its market value at contribution, preventing the same accrued gain from being taxed again.

If value cannot be substantiated or is unreasonable, the tax authority may refer valuation to government price, cost or certification bodies.

## Is annual trust income taxed only when distributed?

It depends on who funded the trust.

### Resident-funded trust

Income of the trust and offshore entities it holds, controls or manages is attributed annually to the resident contributor, whether distributed or not:

- gains and losses from property transfers may offset within the same tax year, but losses cannot be carried forward;
- interest, dividends and other non-transfer income form a separate category;
- the two categories cannot offset each other;
- trustee remuneration, management fees, legal fees and investment-advisory fees are not deductible.

Income already taxed under these rules is not taxed again on later distribution.

### Nonresident-funded trust

A resident beneficiary is generally taxed when receiving a distribution. Merely being named as a beneficiary does not itself create immediate tax.

However, Article 12 deems certain economic benefits to be distributions: unreleased or unpaid year-end loans or guarantees, expenses paid or reimbursed for a resident, free or clearly underpriced use of trust property, and benefits routed through third parties or related persons.

## Does one of four criteria automatically “look through” a BVI company?

No. Definition, control and exceptions must be read together.

An offshore company, partnership, foundation or other organisation may be an “offshore entity” under the announcement if any of these applies:

1. specified passive and low-risk income equals at least 50% of prior-year total profit;
2. employees, registered business address, financial accounting or similar factors fail substantive-operation conditions;
3. organisational funds pay non-business consumption or property expenses for an individual;
4. operating decisions are not actually made by the organisation.

But attribution concerns entities held, controlled or managed by the trust. If the relevant connection is “control”, that term requires either:

- at least 25% direct or indirect equity, voting, unit, income or similar rights; or
- substantive control over funding, operations, purchases and sales, or distributions.

Licensed financial institutions that are locally regulated, independently serve unspecified customers and bear risk, and other organisations that prove a reasonable commercial purpose and substantive operations, are excluded. The taxpayer must substantiate an asserted exception.

“Held” and “managed” are separate connections in the text. The 25% threshold therefore should not be treated as a universal safe harbour for every entity relationship.

## Are PPLI and insurance structures automatically excluded?

No categorical answer is safe.

Article 1 excludes qualifying financial products issued by regulated banks, insurers, securities firms or fund companies, but the conditions are cumulative: local regulation, offering to unspecified customers, and independent business and risk assumption.

A standard retail product and a bespoke private-placement life-insurance structure in which the policyholder retains influence over underlying assets may present materially different facts. The issuer, offering model, underwriting risk, investment direction, account control, beneficiaries and underlying holdings must be reviewed; the word “insurance” is not by itself decisive.

## Does foreign citizenship or permanent residence end Chinese tax residence?

Not necessarily.

Article 11 states that an individual with foreign citizenship or long-term/permanent residence abroad whose principal economic interests originate in China **may** be treated as a domiciled Chinese resident. This is a fact-based “may”, not an automatic rule, and citizenship alone is not determinative.

Article 6 separately requires a resident contributor who becomes nonresident while the trust continues to clear tax on latent trust-property gain and prior unpaid income. This trust-specific exit rule does not by itself establish a general comprehensive Chinese “exit tax”.

## Can foreign tax be credited?

A resident may claim a foreign tax credit for tax of an individual-income-tax nature paid abroad in relation to the trust, subject to China's ordinary credit rules and supporting tax certificates. Corporate income tax paid by an offshore entity is not automatically the resident individual's creditable IIT merely because the entity is trust-related.

## What falls within the 90-day transition?

Announcement No. 21 took effect on 24 July 2026. Article 17 covers:

- unpaid IIT from resident contributions made from 1 January 2023 through 31 December 2025;
- unpaid IIT from nonresident contributions made from 1 January 2023 through the effective date;
- income arising before 1 January 2026 in a resident-funded offshore trust;
- pre-2026 distributions to a resident from a nonresident-funded offshore trust.

Covered amounts must be filed and paid within 90 days of implementation; no late-payment surcharge is imposed within that transition. After the window, ordinary collection, surcharge and—where tax evasion is established—penalty rules apply.

**Boundary:** the contribution rule expressly begins in 2023. The transitional sentence for income of a resident-funded trust does not state the same three-year starting point. Taxpayers should not assume all historical income is automatically limited to three years; the period should be confirmed against the text, available records and the competent tax authority.

## Filing dates and records

| Event | Filing time |
|---|---|
| Resident contribution | 1 March–30 June of the following year |
| Annual resident-funded trust income | 1 March–30 June of the following year |
| Nonresident contribution of China-source property | Within 15 days after month-end |
| Trust termination | Within 15 days after month of completed liquidation |
| Resident becomes nonresident | Within 15 days after month of status change |
| Specified death clearance | Within 15 days after month of death, filed by trustee or designated China entity |

The initial filing generally includes the trust instrument, a schedule of contributed property, the structure chart and other information. A trust established before the announcements must also submit the establishment-year and 2025 annual reports and historical financial statements on its first filing. Foreign-language documents require Chinese translations.

The five-year instalment facility is not a general deferral for annual liabilities. The administrative rules principally make it available, subject to timely filing, for specified liquidation cases—resident-funded trust termination and certain cases following a resident contributor's death.

## Practical self-review

Build four ledgers:

1. **Residence:** annual tax-residence status and supporting evidence;
2. **Contributions:** date, basis, reasonable costs, market value and valuation support;
3. **Income:** annual gains, interest, dividends and other income of the trust and offshore entities;
4. **Benefits:** cash or in-kind distributions, loans, guarantees, expenses, underpriced use and related-party benefits.

Long histories, multiple jurisdictions, PPLI, protectors, reserved powers, mixed contributors or missing accounts warrant professional review and confirmation with the competent tax authority before filing.
