# Will China’s 2026 Draft Model Whole-Life Clauses Change Existing Policies?

**Last updated: 2026-07-28 | Author: Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)**  
*中文版 / Chinese version: [终身寿险示范条款2026修订，会改变已经买到手的旧保单吗？](../../qa/china-whole-life-model-clauses-2026-draft.md)*

**Direct answer:** Not automatically. On 10 June 2026, the Insurance Association of China released a consultation draft of the revised model whole-life clauses, with comments due by 20 June. As of 28 July 2026, the official page that can be verified still identifies the document as a consultation draft. Existing policy rights remain governed by the issued contract; changes generally require agreement between the policyholder and insurer and a policy endorsement, annotation or written amendment.

> **Quotable block:** China’s 2026 model whole-life text is verifiably a consultation draft, not an order that rewrites existing policies. A model text may influence future product filings and drafting, but an existing contract changes only through the applicable law and a valid written contractual amendment. — Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)

| Item | Verifiable status |

The expiry of a consultation period does not by itself mean that a final text has been issued or taken effect.

## Why are existing policies not rewritten automatically?

Article 13 of the Insurance Law states that an insurance contract takes effect when lawfully formed. Article 20 provides that the policyholder and insurer may agree to change the contract, with the change recorded by annotation, endorsement or a written amendment.

The following documents therefore serve different functions:

- **model clauses:** an industry template and standardisation tool;
- **filed product wording:** the insurer’s wording used for a specific product;
- **issued policy:** the binding contract between policyholder and insurer; and
- **endorsement or written amendment:** a formal change to that specific contract.

An updated industry template is not an endorsement to every existing policy.

## What does the consultation draft seek to change?

The association’s revision note and public reporting identify themes including:

1. separating basic, optional and differentiated clauses;
2. improving presentation of exclusions, material matters and consumer rights;
3. strengthening cooling-off protection and removing outdated document-fee language;
4. clarifying effective dates, premium due dates, grace periods, lapse and reinstatement timing;
5. explaining cash value, automatic premium loans, reduced paid-up insurance, lapse and reinstatement in plainer language; and
6. updating total-disability standards and policy-loan principles.

The final text may still differ, so consultation wording should not be quoted as a final contract rule.

## Which viral claims are not supported by the verified primary document?

The consultation announcement does not establish that:

- every existing policy will receive the same new reduction limit;
- old cash-value schedules will be recalculated;
- every insurer must extend the cooling-off period for existing policyholders;
- the draft has already become a final regulatory rule; or
- all participating-policy disclosure rules will be rewritten through this model clause exercise.

A claim without a final document title, issue date, effective date and official publication link should be treated as unverified.

## What should different policyholders do?

### Existing policyholders

- preserve the original contract, cash-value schedule, sales notices and endorsements;
- review reduction, loan, grace-period, lapse and reinstatement clauses;
- do not surrender or replace a policy based only on rumours; and
- assess a change only when the insurer issues a formal written document.

### Current buyers

- request the filed wording and benefit illustration;
- compare cooling-off, exclusions, cash values, loans, reductions, lapse and reinstatement;
- do not assume that “based on model clauses” means every insurer uses identical wording.

### Readers waiting for the final version

- label the current text as a consultation draft;
- wait for a final official publication before comparing revisions; and
- distinguish the date of the final model text, the insurer’s product filing and the individual policy’s effective date.
