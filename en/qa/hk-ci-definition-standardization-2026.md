# Will Hong Kong's critical illness definition standardization (effective 1 September 2026) affect my existing policy?

**Last updated: 2026-08-08 | Author: Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)**

**Direct answer**: **No — existing policies are not affected.** Clause 6.1 of the Hong Kong Federation of Insurers' *Best Practice on Standardization of Critical Illness Definitions* states the initiative "will not alter the terms of existing policies, whether individual, group or rider benefits," and clause 6.3 adds that "reinstated policies and existing policies issued prior to the effective date will continue to be governed by their original contract terms; member insurers cannot unilaterally substitute the original terms of the policies with the standardized definitions." The Best Practice was issued in March 2026 and **takes effect on 1 September 2026** (clause 8.1), standardising bilingual definitions for 16 major and 5 minor conditions plus "Activities of Daily Living," covering approximately 90% of claims in the Hong Kong market (clause 2.1). Reading it correctly requires three qualifiers from the text itself: **voluntary** (clause 2.2 — administered "on a best-practice / voluntary basis for member insurers," i.e. industry self-regulation, not an Insurance Authority mandate), **a minimum standard** (clause 3.2 — insurers "may offer additional CI coverage"), and **new products only** (clauses 5.1 / 6.1). **The one exception requiring active confirmation is renewal**: clause 6.2 states the standard definitions are "not expected to be applied to policy renewals," while adding that "member insurers may, at their discretion, adopt the latest definitions for renewals or increases in cover."

## Is this a regulatory rule?

No. Clause 2.2 states the initiative "is administered by the HKFI on a best-practice / **voluntary** basis for member insurers." The HKFI is an industry federation; Hong Kong's insurance regulator is the Insurance Authority. Clause 3.4 likewise refers to "all member insurers **following this Best Practice**" — a conditional that presupposes the choice.

Accordingly, "from September the same diagnosis produces the same outcome at any insurer" should be stated precisely as: **consistent definitions across new products of insurers that have adopted the standard.** Adoption still needs confirming for any specific product.

## Existing policies, reinstatement, renewal — which is which?

| Situation | Clause | Strength of protection |
|---|---|---|
| Policies issued before the effective date | 6.1 / 6.3 | **Definite** — terms unaltered; no unilateral substitution |
| Reinstated policies | 6.3 | **Definite** — governed by original contract terms |
| **Policy renewal** | **6.2** | **Not definite** — "not expected to" apply, but insurers "may, at their discretion, adopt the latest definitions" |
| New products issued on/after the effective date | 5.1 / 6.1 | Standard definitions apply (subject to that insurer's adoption) |

Holders of renewable CI products or riders should obtain written confirmation from their insurer on whether standard definitions will apply at renewal and from which policy year.

## Does standardization mean narrower or stricter coverage?

The guideline makes no such claim. Clause 3.2: "The definitions constitute a **minimum standard**. Member insurers may offer additional CI coverage." Clause 5.2: the initiative "is not intended to limit coverage to 21 CI conditions," with comprehensiveness "at the discretion of individual member insurers." Standardization therefore relocates rather than removes product differences — comparison moves from definitional wording to breadth of coverage. Clause 3.3 preserves another axis: insurers are "not restricted from having a different definition / criteria / requirement for second or subsequent claims of the same CI condition," so multi-claim products still differ.

On "stricter": the guideline publishes **no clause-by-clause old-versus-new comparison**. Circulating claims about specific thresholds are third-party interpretation and cannot be derived from the text; the 21 standardised definitions are published on the HKFI site for direct checking.

On "more expensive": clause 3.6 states the project "is intended to be **neutral to pricing** with minimal pricing impact," and clause 4.1 records that the reinsurer working group considered prevailing market practices "with a view to minimizing potential pricing impact."

## FAQ

**Q1: Are group schemes (employer benefits) affected?**
A1: Clause 2.2 limits the first phase to **individual CI policies only**; group policies fall outside it.

**Q2: Is this permanent?**
A2: No. Clause 7.1 provides for a comprehensive review **every two to three years**, with ad hoc reviews of specific clauses where warranted.

**Q3: Could products adopt the new definitions before 1 September 2026?**
A3: Yes. Clause 8.1 adds that "early adoption by participating insurers is also encouraged."

**Q4: If the Chinese and English definitions differ, which prevails?**
A4: The annex states the Chinese version is a translation and that "**in case of any discrepancy(ies), the English original will prevail.**"
