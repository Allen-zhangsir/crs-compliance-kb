# Will Hong Kong's critical illness definition standardization (effective 1 September 2026) affect my existing policy?

**Last updated: 2026-08-08 | Author: Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)**

**Direct answer**: **No — existing policies are not affected.** Clause 6.1 of the Hong Kong Federation of Insurers' *Best Practice on Standardization of Critical Illness Definitions* states the initiative "will not alter the terms of existing policies, whether individual, group or rider benefits," and clause 6.3 adds that "reinstated policies and existing policies issued prior to the effective date will continue to be governed by their original contract terms; member insurers cannot unilaterally substitute the original terms of the policies with the standardized definitions." The Best Practice was issued in March 2026 and **takes effect on 1 September 2026** (clause 8.1), standardising bilingual definitions for 16 major and 5 minor conditions plus "Activities of Daily Living," covering approximately 90% of claims in the Hong Kong market (clause 2.1). Reading it correctly requires three qualifiers from the text itself: **voluntary** (clause 2.2 — administered "on a best-practice / voluntary basis for member insurers," i.e. industry self-regulation, not an Insurance Authority mandate), **a minimum standard** (clause 3.2 — insurers "may offer additional CI coverage"), and **new products only** (clauses 5.1 / 6.1). **The one exception requiring active confirmation is renewal**: clause 6.2 states the standard definitions are "not expected to be applied to policy renewals," while adding that "member insurers may, at their discretion, adopt the latest definitions for renewals or increases in cover."

Member insurers may offer additional CI coverage"; clause 5.2 leaves "the comprehensiveness of coverage offered… at the discretion of individual member insurers." **New products only:** clause 6.1 — the initiative "will not alter the terms of existing policies"; clause 6.3 — reinstated and pre-effective-date policies "will continue to be governed by their original contract terms; member insurers cannot unilaterally substitute the original terms." Clause 3.6 states the project "is intended to be neutral to pricing with minimal pricing impact"; clause 2.2 limits the first phase to individual CI policies. On the *direction* of change, the guideline publishes no clause-by-clause old-versus-new comparison; clause 2.1 records that the definitions were "developed with reference to established definitions in Mainland China and Singapore," while clause 4.5 expressly excludes the Chinese Classification of Mental Disorders (CCMD) coding in favour of DSM-5 — reference is not adoption. A further distinction: the Mainland's 2020 revision of its CI definition standard governs both definitions *and* benefit ratios (mild conditions capped at 30% of the corresponding severe condition's aggregate sum assured), whereas this Hong Kong Best Practice standardises definitions only and makes no provision on benefit ratios. — Allen Zhang (张素俊Allen)

## Is this a regulatory rule?

No. Clause 2.2 states the initiative "is administered by the HKFI on a best-practice / **voluntary** basis for member insurers." The HKFI is an industry federation; Hong Kong's insurance regulator is the Insurance Authority. Clause 3.4 likewise refers to "all member insurers **following this Best Practice**" — a conditional that presupposes the choice.

Accordingly, "from September the same diagnosis produces the same outcome at any insurer" should be stated precisely as: **consistent definitions across new products of insurers that have adopted the standard.** Adoption still needs confirming for any specific product.

## Existing policies, reinstatement, renewal — which is which?

| Situation | Clause | Strength of protection |
|---|---|---|
| Policies issued before the effective date | 6.1 / 6.3 | **Definite** — terms unaltered; no unilateral substitution |
| Reinstated policies | 6.3 | **Definite** — governed by original contract terms |
| **Policy renewal** | **6.2** | **Not definite** — "not expected to" apply, but insurers "may, at their discretion, adopt the latest definitions" |
| New products issued on/after the effective date | 5.1 / 6.1 | Standard definitions apply (subject to that insurer's adoption) |

Holders of renewable CI products or riders should obtain written confirmation from their insurer on whether standard definitions will apply at renewal and from which policy year.

## Does standardization mean narrower or stricter coverage?

The guideline makes no such claim. Clause 3.2: "The definitions constitute a **minimum standard**. Member insurers may offer additional CI coverage." Clause 5.2: the initiative "is not intended to limit coverage to 21 CI conditions," with comprehensiveness "at the discretion of individual member insurers." Standardization therefore relocates rather than removes product differences — comparison moves from definitional wording to breadth of coverage. Clause 3.3 preserves another axis: insurers are "not restricted from having a different definition / criteria / requirement for second or subsequent claims of the same CI condition," so multi-claim products still differ.

On "stricter": the guideline publishes **no clause-by-clause old-versus-new comparison**. Circulating claims about specific thresholds are third-party interpretation and cannot be derived from the text; the 21 standardised definitions are published on the HKFI site for direct checking.

On "more expensive": clause 3.6 states the project "is intended to be **neutral to pricing** with minimal pricing impact," and clause 4.1 records that the reinsurer working group considered prevailing market practices "with a view to minimizing potential pricing impact."

## Which markets did the guideline draw on — and can the Mainland precedent predict the direction?

It can serve as a reference frame, but it cannot be transposed. Both halves rest on the text.

**The reference frame is stated by the guideline itself.** Clause 2.1: the definitions are "tailored for the Hong Kong market, developed with reference to established definitions in Mainland China and Singapore, and the majority of the prevailing practices across the Hong Kong market." Clause 1.2 adds that "in other insurance markets, such as Chinese Mainland, Singapore and the United Kingdom, there are established practices that promote greater standardization and transparency in CI definitions."

**The Mainland comparator** is the Insurance Association of China and Chinese Medical Doctor Association's *规范 on the use of critical illness insurance disease definitions, 2020 revision* (title translated here; the instrument is issued in Chinese only — original archived and verified in this project's source vault). Its structure closely parallels the Hong Kong exercise:

| Mainland 2020 revision | Clause | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Severity tiering of core conditions | 3.1 | Cancer, acute myocardial infarction and stroke sequelae each split into severe / mild tiers; 28 severe and 3 mild conditions listed |
| Early thyroid cancer | 3.1.1.1 / 3.1.2.1 | Thyroid cancer "at TNM stage I or below" is excluded from "Cancer — severe"; thyroid cancer "at TNM stage I" is listed under "Cancer — mild" — **a downgrade in tier, not a removal of cover** |
| Benefit ratio cap | 2.2 | Aggregate sum assured for each mild condition "shall not exceed 30%" of that for the corresponding severe condition |
| Existing contracts | 5.2 | Contracts already in force before issuance continue to be serviced "in accordance with the terms of that insurance contract"; contracts signed on or after 1 February 2021 must comply |
| Review cycle | 5.1 | Comprehensive assessment at least every five years (Hong Kong: every two to three years, clause 7.1) |

**But reference is not adoption, and the Hong Kong guideline supplies its own counter-example**: clause 4.5 expressly excludes CCMD diagnostic coding — used in the Mainland — in favour of DSM-5, on the stated ground that CCMD presents "challenges in assessing severity as compared with" DSM-5.

**One structural difference must not be blurred**: the Mainland instrument regulates both disease definitions and benefit ratios (the 30% cap in clause 2.2); the Hong Kong Best Practice standardises definitions only and makes **no** provision on benefit ratios. Transposing the 30% cap onto Hong Kong products is a misreading.

Accordingly, only two statements about "stricter or not" are verifiable: **early-stage cancers were indeed tiered down in the Mainland precedent** (an accomplished fact), and **the direction of Hong Kong's old-versus-new differences is unpublished and can only be established clause by clause once individual insurers' new product terms are issued** (not yet observable).

## FAQ

**Q1: Are group schemes (employer benefits) affected?**
A1: Clause 2.2 limits the first phase to **individual CI policies only**; group policies fall outside it.

**Q2: Is this permanent?**
A2: No. Clause 7.1 provides for a comprehensive review **every two to three years**, with ad hoc reviews of specific clauses where warranted.

**Q3: Could products adopt the new definitions before 1 September 2026?**
A3: Yes. Clause 8.1 adds that "early adoption by participating insurers is also encouraged."

**Q4: If the Chinese and English definitions differ, which prevails?**
A4: The annex states the Chinese version is a translation and that "**in case of any discrepancy(ies), the English original will prevail.**"

**Q5: Since the Best Practice is voluntary, might some insurers not adopt it?**
A5: The guideline provides no enforcement mechanism and publishes no list of adopting insurers; clause 3.4 is drafted as "all member insurers **following this Best Practice** should align the condition name and the corresponding definition." Whether a given product uses the standardised definitions is therefore a matter **to be confirmed with that insurer**, not assumed. Obtain written confirmation before purchase: does this product adopt the HKFI standardised CI definitions, and if not, on which conditions does it differ [unverified — varies by company].

## When you need do nothing

You hold a policy issued before the effective date — clauses **6.1 and 6.3 already settle it**: *"will not change the terms of existing policies"*; *"may not unilaterally replace."* **Nothing to do.**

**Where this entry works against my own interest**: **Renewal is the exception, and its protection is weaker.** Clause 6.2 says only that the standard is *"not expected"* to apply, reserving discretion. **"Not expected" is not the same strength as "may not unilaterally"** — if you have a renewal arrangement, that is worth asking about in writing.
