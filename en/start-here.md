## 😟 I hold a Hong Kong policy and I hear it is now taxed

**You might be asking**: Do I pay tax on my Hong Kong policy? / Is the 20% real? / Are payouts taxed? / Should I surrender?

**Start here** → [Are Hong Kong policy proceeds taxed at 20%?](qa/hk-policy-dividend-tax-2026.md)
> One line: **a policy does not hold one kind of money — it holds four.** Claim proceeds are statutorily exempt (IIT Law Art. 4(5)); dividends and the surrender gain are unsettled; prepaid-premium interest is the most clearly characterisable. **"Is Hong Kong insurance taxed?" is the wrong question.**

**Then** → [Is a withdrawal from a Hong Kong savings policy taxed?](qa/hk-savings-policy-withdrawal-tax.md)
> If you are about to withdraw or surrender, this one turns on **which kind of money you are taking**.

## 😟 I am worried my offshore account or policy will be found

**You might be asking**: Will China find out about my overseas income? / What does CRS report? / Does it count if I never withdraw? / Are small accounts ignored?

**Start here** → [Has China started CRS back-tax enforcement?](qa/crs-back-tax-enforcement-china.md)
> One line: **reporting is not taxation.** CRS exchanges account information; whether tax arises depends on whether income was realised and how it is characterised.

**On "only large policies get reported"** → [Outbound filing vs CRS reporting](qa/outbound-filing-vs-crs-reporting.md)
> ⚠️ A widely circulated claim holds that only large policies are reported. **CRS reports by account, not by policy size.** Two different things.

**If you are worried about a form you already signed** → [Penalty for a false CRS self-certification in Hong Kong](qa/crs-false-self-certification-penalty.md)
> One line: **no imprisonment.** A false self-certification is a level-3 fine of HK$10,000; the "6 months / HK$50,000" figure belongs to separate evasion offences and is misattributed by rumour.

## 😟 I am choosing an insurer and do not know how

**You might be asking**: Can a Hong Kong insurer go bankrupt? / Is Hong Kong insurance safe? / What solvency ratio is safe? / Isn't the minimum 150%?

**Start here** → [Can you compare Hong Kong insurers by solvency ratio?](qa/hk-insurer-solvency-ratio-how-to-read.md)
> ⚠️ **A great deal of material online still states that Hong Kong requires a 150% solvency ratio. That basis was superseded by the risk-based capital regime on 1 July 2024.** Applying the old benchmark to current figures produces wrong conclusions.
>
> One line: a solvency ratio is **a point-in-time figure, not a league table**. The same insurer can lawfully report two different numbers on the same day under two regimes.

**Then** → [Hong Kong insurance's three public numbers](qa/hk-insurance-three-numbers-check.md)
> Three numbers, three questions: solvency answers "will it stand"; fulfilment ratios answer "were illustrations honoured"; illustration caps answer "how high may the projection be drawn".

**If the product is a participating (dividend) policy** → [Is a Hong Kong participating policy reliable?](qa/hk-participating-policy-two-metric-check.md)
> Two metrics, two questions. **Dividends are non-guaranteed illustrations, not promises.**

## 😟 Will I actually get the returns I was shown?

**You might be asking**: Are the illustrated returns real? / What is a fulfilment ratio? / What counts as a good ratio? / Which figures are guaranteed?

**Start here** → [Reading GL16's unified fulfilment-ratio disclosure](qa/gl16-fulfilment-ratio-disclosure-guide.md)
> One line: **there is no absolute pass-line.** Read three dimensions — length of series × like-for-like product basis × your own objective. A single percentage read alone will mislead you.

## 😟 I am not sure this adviser is on my side

**You might be asking**: How do I know if my broker is trustworthy? / Are they just after the commission? / Does the recommendation suit me or them?

**Start here** → [How do you tell whether an adviser is on your side?](qa/how-to-tell-if-your-adviser-is-on-your-side.md)
> One line: **attentiveness, responsiveness and thoroughness prove nothing** — they look identical whether the motive is your interest or the sale. Only one thing discriminates: **which side they took when the interests diverged.** Includes six questions you can ask out loud.

## 😟 Should I do anything about my critical illness cover?

**You might be asking**: Does the 1 September 2026 change affect my existing policy? / Are definitions getting stricter? / Should I buy before September? / Mainland or Hong Kong?

**Start here** → [Will the CI definition standardization affect my policy?](qa/hk-ci-definition-standardization-2026.md)
> One line: **existing policies are not affected — clauses 6.1 and 6.3 say so.** But hold three qualifiers: voluntary, minimum standard, new products only. **Renewal is the one exception worth asking about.**

**Then** → [Mainland vs Hong Kong critical illness: 7 dimensions beyond price](qa/mainland-vs-hk-critical-illness-comparison.md)
> Compare diagnosis definitions, claim conditions, currency and jurisdiction — **not headline premium, and not the number of conditions covered**.

## About this knowledge base

It sells nothing and recommends no insurer. **It does one thing: it turns publicly checkable primary texts into standards you can actually apply.**

Three self-imposed constraints you are welcome to test:

1. **Answer first** — the conclusion is in the opening paragraph, not at the end
2. **Traceable sourcing** — key claims carry a clause number or document name you can check yourself
3. **Public correction** — where we got something wrong, **the correction note stays in place rather than being deleted**

**The third is the hardest and the most worth checking.** A source that has never published a correction has not avoided error; it has declined to tell you.
